Reflection No. 04 · Learning Together

Different minds.
One direction.

My reflection on group work, shared responsibility and the lessons I am learning about working with other people as a Business Intelligence and Data Analytics student.

✦ Personal development ✦ Communication ✦ Collaboration

Inside this reflection

Growth happens between people.

Group work is more than dividing tasks. It asks me to listen, contribute, adapt and take responsibility for something bigger than my individual effort.

“I can learn from other people's perspectives without losing the value of my own.”

Before the five stages

Understanding my place in a team

As a first-year Business Intelligence and Data Analytics student at Botswana Accountancy College, I am learning that working with other people is an important part of academic and professional development. In this field, a person may understand data individually, but communicating findings, comparing ideas and solving problems with others are also essential.

I consider myself hardworking, curious and willing to challenge myself. However, I also recognise that group work requires more than personal determination. I need to make room for other opinions, communicate clearly, respect different working styles and remember that every member has a role to play.

The five stages discussed in the TAC manual give me a useful structure for reflecting on how a group can develop, how I can contribute at different points and what I can improve in future collaborative assignments.

“Being a good team member means bringing my own strengths while making space for everyone else's.” My personal approach to collaboration
✦

Stage one

Forming

The forming stage represents the beginning of a group's journey. Members are getting to know one another, trying to understand the task and working out how they will cooperate. At this point, I would want to understand the assignment properly before rushing into completing my part.

I know that I can be focused on getting things done, especially when I want to do well. However, I am learning that starting quickly is not always the same as starting effectively. I need to listen carefully, ask questions when something is unclear and give other members an opportunity to share their ideas.

How I can contribute

I can help the group establish a clear direction by discussing the task requirements, agreeing on what needs to be achieved and making sure everyone understands their responsibilities. I would also try to create a respectful atmosphere in which members feel comfortable participating.

My key lesson

A strong beginning depends on communication, clear expectations and a willingness to understand the people I will be working with.

↗

Stage two

Storming

The storming stage reminds me that group work does not always move smoothly. Members may have different ideas, expectations, priorities or ways of completing a task. These differences can make collaboration challenging, especially when everyone wants their contribution to be understood.

I am learning that being hardworking does not mean I will always have the best approach. Even when I feel confident about an idea, I need to consider whether another person's perspective could improve it. If I disagree with someone, I should focus on the issue rather than making the discussion personal.

How I can respond

I can practise patience, listen before responding and explain my reasoning respectfully. If responsibilities or expectations are unclear, I should encourage the group to discuss the problem directly instead of allowing confusion to continue. I also need to be willing to reconsider my position when another idea is supported by good reasoning.

My key lesson

Disagreement does not have to divide a team. When handled respectfully, different viewpoints can help us question assumptions and produce better work.

◎

Stage three

Norming

Norming is the stage in which a group begins to develop shared expectations and more consistent ways of working. Members start understanding how they can support one another, communicate progress and take responsibility for their individual tasks.

I see this as an important stage because good teamwork requires trust. I cannot expect other people to rely on me if I do not communicate, respect agreed deadlines or take ownership of my responsibilities. In the same way, I need to respect the contributions that other members bring to the group.

How I can contribute

I can support this stage by keeping communication clear, completing my allocated work carefully and asking for clarification when necessary. I can also encourage the group to agree on deadlines, methods of sharing information and ways to check that all parts of the assignment fit together.

My key lesson

Trust develops through consistent actions. Being dependable means following through on my responsibilities and respecting the responsibilities of others.

✺

Stage four

Performing

The performing stage focuses on the group's ability to work towards its objectives with greater cooperation and purpose. Members understand their roles and can concentrate more effectively on solving problems and completing the task.

As a Business Intelligence and Data Analytics student, I can see how important this is. A group project may involve researching information, analysing data, preparing visualisations and presenting findings. If each person works separately without communicating, the final result may feel disconnected. The different contributions need to support one another.

I want to develop the ability to contribute my strengths while remaining aware of the group's overall objective. This means doing my work carefully, communicating progress, checking accuracy and offering support when a teammate needs clarification. It also means recognising that the quality of the final work belongs to the team, not just to the individual who completed a particular section.

How I can contribute

I can focus on the quality of my work, communicate useful ideas, remain open to feedback and help ensure that our separate contributions form one clear and consistent outcome.

My key lesson

Effective teamwork is not simply a collection of individual achievements. It is the ability to combine different strengths towards a shared result.

❋

Stage five

Adjourning

Adjourning represents the conclusion of the group's work. Once the task has been completed or presented, it is important to look back at the process rather than focusing only on the final outcome. This gives me an opportunity to recognise what went well, what could have been handled differently and what I have learned about myself as a team member.

I would want to reflect honestly on whether I communicated effectively, managed my responsibilities and gave other members enough space to contribute. I would also consider whether I handled different opinions maturely and whether I asked for help when I needed it.

For me, reflection is valuable because I do not want to repeat the same mistakes simply because an assignment is finished. Each group task can help me become more organised, confident and thoughtful in the way I work with others.

How I can conclude well

I can acknowledge the contributions of the team, review the quality of the completed work and identify practical lessons to carry into future assignments. I can also appreciate that different people may contribute in different ways.

My key lesson

Completing a task is one achievement; understanding what the experience taught me is another. I want to carry those lessons into my future studies and working life.

Looking ahead

What I want to carry forward

Reflecting on these five stages helps me understand that collaboration is a skill I can continue developing. I do not have to be perfect at every part of group work immediately. What matters is that I remain willing to learn, take responsibility for my actions and make improvements as I gain experience.

In my future career, especially if I work as a Data Analyst in the banking industry, I will need to communicate findings, understand different perspectives and work with people who have different responsibilities and expertise. The habits I develop through academic group work can help prepare me for that environment.

01 · Communicate Share my ideas clearly and listen with an open mind.
02 · Be accountable Complete my responsibilities and keep others informed.
03 · Stay adaptable Accept constructive feedback and consider different solutions.
04 · Keep improving Reflect on each experience and apply what I learn next time.
