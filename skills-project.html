Proposed learning project

Understanding customer service in banking.

One area I would like to explore is how a bank could use customer feedback to understand service quality. Customer feedback may contain useful information about waiting times, communication, digital banking and the overall customer experience.

For a learning project, I could work with a suitable sample dataset, organise the records in Excel, compare recurring themes and create charts that summarise the findings. I would then explain what the results might mean and what further questions a bank could investigate.

This project connects with my ambition to become a Data Analyst in the banking industry. It would also help me practise analysing information from a business perspective rather than looking at numbers without considering the problem they are meant to solve.

My analytical mindset

Numbers need a story.

I want to develop the habit of asking meaningful questions before drawing conclusions. Data becomes useful when it is examined carefully, interpreted in context and communicated in a way that helps someone understand a problem.

I also need to remember that a pattern is not automatically an explanation. Good analysis involves checking the quality of information, considering limitations and being honest about what the evidence does and does not show.

01

Start with a question

Identify the business problem before deciding which information to collect or examine.

02

Prepare the information

Check for missing, inconsistent or duplicated records before using data to draw conclusions.

03

Look for meaningful patterns

Compare categories, examine trends and use suitable visualisations to make findings easier to understand.

04

Explain the evidence

Present findings clearly, acknowledge limitations and distinguish evidence from assumptions.
